\section{Background}

One of the main purposes of Artificial Intelligence (AI) methods has been to automate tasks. When a new AI method is developed, users test it in different tasks to determine what can now be automated. Reviews on the use of AI in Ecology offer a summary of how these methods are used across the field and, as such, are often used by researchers to understand the overall state of AI use in Ecology. However, with the increase in the volume of scientific research (primary literature), there has also been an increase in the number of reviews (secondary literature). Such an increase presents a challenge to staying up to date on the topic. Also, given the field's breadth, a researcher interested in an overall summary of the subject would need to review several reviews. These issues can be solved by a review of reviews, which provides an overarching view of the evidence collected by reviews \cite{biondi2016umbrella}. As with systematic reviews, a review of reviews includes a critical appraisal of the included reviews. The critical appraisal is important to identify any variability in the methodologies used in the included reviews, because disregarding these differences risks providing a distorted, misinformed view of the field.

\section{Objectives}
In this study, we assess the secondary literature, specifically systematic reviews and systematic maps (not including narrative reviews), on AI methods in the field of Ecology. Our approach combines systematic mapping with appraisal and bibliometric analysis. 

We have three main objectives:
\ben
\item Reveal trends in the coverage, in the systematic reviews and maps, of the different AI methods used in the field of Ecology, and identify any knowledge gaps and biases. 
\item Perform a critical appraisal of the systematic reviews and maps to assess their reporting quality and identify their strengths and shortcomings. 
\item Quantify the reach and impact of the systematic reviews and maps in the field through bibliometric analyzes, including locations of origin (e.g., affiliations, countries), collaboration patterns, and citations.

\section{Inclusion and exclusion criteria}
\label{sec:criteria}
\subsection{Population}
Inclusion: reviews must focus on AI (or Machine Learning and Deep Learning) applications in any biological system, including organismal, or ecological levels. Studies on mixed domains are included if biological applications are a substantial focus.

Exclusion: Exclude reviews where the analytical focus is exclusively on non-biological systems (e.g., finance, engineering, computer vision without a biological application) or on humans for purely clinical/biomedical purposes, or application in biological systems on the molecular or cellular levels.

\subsection{Exposure}
Inclusion: Reviews must explicitly analyze Machine Learning (ML), Deep Learning (DL), or Artificial Intelligence (AI) as a distinct set of tools. This includes supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning methods.

Exclusion: Exclude reviews focusing solely on traditional statistical methods (e.g., linear regression, ANOVA) without framing them in the context of AI. Exclude reviews where AI is not a central theme or cannot be distinguished from other computational methods. Exclude reviews focusing only on data collection for remote sensing.

\subsection{Comparator}
Not applicable

\subsection{Outcome}
Inclusion: The review must discuss the outcomes of applying AI, such as prediction, classification, clustering, or generation of biological data. This includes outcomes such as modeling ecological systems or classifying species from images.

Exclusion: Exclude studies focused only on the theoretical or mathematical aspects of AI algorithms without discussing their application or impact in a biological context. Exclude papers on software development that do not review the outcomes of its use.

\subsection{Study type}
Inclusion: Only secondary literature, specifically systematic reviews and systematic maps that synthesize primary literature, will be included (meta-analyses will also be included, although such studies are not expected). Studies must be peer-reviewed.

Exclusion: Exclude primary research articles, editorials, opinion pieces, book chapters, and narrative/scoping reviews.

\section{Screening}

Article selection will be conducted for all pooled bibliographic records from the two databases searched (Web of Science and Scopus), after duplicate entries are removed and transferred to SysRev. The screening will be carried out in two phases. First, we will screen based on Title, Abstract, and Keywords following our inclusion and exclusion criteria defined in Section \ref{sec:criteria}. Following this, we will collect the full texts of the selected articles and perform a second selection based on the full texts, using the same criteria as in the first screening. This will result in the papers on which we will perform data extraction.

To minimize biased selection in the screening process, an independent dual screening process will be implemented in both screening phases. Discrepancies will be resolved through discussion or, if necessary, adjudicated by a third independent reviewer. The main points of disagreement will be documented.

\subsection{Pilot of literature screening}

We conducted a two-person pilot of the screening and data extraction procedures to evaluate the inclusion and exclusion criteria and the availability of the data to be extracted. We selected a random sample of $100$ articles from the Web of Science search results. We first screened this sample based on the titles and abstracts of the papers, resulting in 6 provisionally included articles. For the provisionally included papers from the first screening, we did a second screening round based on the full text, from which we included two. Based on these results, we expect around $40$ systematic reviews and systematic maps to be included in our review of reviews.

\subsection{Data extraction pilot}
We conducted a data-extraction pilot on the papers that made it through the screening process. We evaluated whether the desired data points can be extracted from the papers and adjusted accordingly. We also estimated the time for data extraction for each type of review as the average time it took to do the extraction on the papers that are in our benchmark. The average was $14.5$ minutes. We expected to perform data extraction on 60 reviews, so we estimated it would take $870$ minutes.
